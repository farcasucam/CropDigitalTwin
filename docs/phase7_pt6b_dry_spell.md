# Phase 7 / PT-6b: an outdoor dry spell with irrigation as a player

Status: DEVELOPMENT. Tomato, outdoor (no controller), window 336 h from hour 2000, four held-out DEV seasons (34, 35, 37, 38). Script `manual_phase7_pt6b_dry_spell.py`, report `data/xai/effects_pt6b_dry_spell.json`; reading rules fixed in the script's docstring before the run. The first, flawed run is kept as `data/xai/effects_pt6b_dry_spell_v0_hour2300_irrigation_0p3.json` (see below).

## What exploration found before the design (one season, no inference)
* **The `IRRIGATION` event replaces the plan's irrigation** during its interval with the stated amount times about 0.95 (it does not add to it). The outdoor plan is a fixed 0.38 mm per hour. An amount of 0.3 therefore reduced the irrigation; a remedy needs more than 0.38 (0.6 here). My first design ("extra irrigation 0.3") was a deficit with the wrong name.
* **The windows where water limits and where the crop grows barely overlap.** At hours 1700 and 1850 (growing) there is almost no water stress; at 2000 (growth tapering) a heat wave plus a radiation spell give about 250 h of stress; from hour 2150 growth is over (growth factor 0, biomass flat). The first run, at hour 2300, therefore gave exactly zero biomass effect for every player (report v0, kept; its water-stress cells came from the deficit irrigation).
* The synthetic weather has no rain at all.

## Results of the corrected run (four seasons; ranges)
* **Biomass (total and fruit).** The total effect of the three players is -11.8 to -14.0 g/m2 (fruit -2.8 to -4.4). Shapley shares: heat wave about two thirds, radiation spell about 30 percent, extra irrigation 0.08 to 0.10 g/m2, **0.6 to 1.1 percent** of the sum of absolute shares. Irrigation is not a cause of the biomass here even though it removes the water stress. The interaction (heat wave with radiation, both raising demand) is **34 to 46 percent** of the sum of absolute shares, larger than outdoors in PT-6a.
* **Data method (told the applied irrigation), biomass.** Top player right in 4 of 4 (total) and 3 to 4 of 4 (fruit), sign right 8 of 8, normalised error 0.16 to 0.46, explained fraction 0.51 to 0.69 for the total (0.35 to 1.07 for the fruit): ranking and sign right, about half of the magnitude, as in PT-6a.
* **Hours with water stress above 0.05.** The pre-declared rule marks the four cells as NOT material: the total effect of the three players against the reference is 0 h, because the irrigation removes exactly the stress that the heat wave and the radiation spell cause. The data method's total is -5 to +13 h, so the rule counts "false effects 4/4" on a truth that is zero in total. That label is misleading here: the truth's shares are large and opposite (heat wave +23 to +35 h, radiation +26 to +69 h, irrigation -50 to -96 h), the same cancelling design that inflated the PT-3b metrics.
* **Post-hoc, NOT pre-declared (read from the report): the shares of the water-stress hours.** The data method's irrigation share is -15 to +2 h against a true -50 to -96 h (the sign is wrong in 2 of 8 cells, the magnitude is about a tenth), and its top player agrees in 1 of 4 cells with the oracle baseline and 1 of 4 with climatology; its normalised error is 0.39 to 0.51. A method that observes the applied irrigation and the weather does not recover what the irrigation compensated, because in its training episodes the stress was rarely large enough to learn it.

## Reading
* For the growth outcome the twin says something simple and strong: the weather cost about 13 g/m2 and the irrigation, however effective against stress, did not change it. A data method gets the order and the sign and about half of the size.
* For the stress outcome the useful explanation is a compensation: the heat and the radiation caused 50 to 100 h of stress each season and the irrigation removed all of it. This is the kind of statement a net contrast hides (total 0) and the data method misses; it needs the effect of each player, not the total, and the normaliser of the shares (sum of absolute shares), which PT-3b already proposed for the confirmatory protocol.
* The test also shows two properties of the twin that matter for the next work package: the water and growth models are decoupled in time in this season (stress where there is no growth), and the irrigation event semantics (replace, not add) must be part of the effect contract.

## What it does not say
One crop, one generator, one window per season, three players, segments given, a simple surrogate trained on 24 episodes. The window was chosen after looking at one season of exploration (disclosed above), not at the evaluation seasons' results. No fertilisation player.
