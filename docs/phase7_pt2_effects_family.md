# Phase 7 / PT-2: a family of nine episodes and how much the division depends on the choices that define it

Status: DEVELOPMENT. Nine cells of one synthetic generator (3 crops x 3 seeds, each seed a different synthetic weather), greenhouse with the climate controller on, a 14-day window. They are not an independent sample of anything: no inferential test, no confirmatory claim, no statement about greenhouses in general. The twin's crop model is uncalibrated and the equipment is illustrative.
Scripts: `manual_phase7_pt2_effects_family.py` (family and four sensitivities; reports in `data/xai/effects_pt2/`, `data/xai/effects_pt2_summary.json`, hash 7fd98abd…), `manual_phase7_pt2b_grouping_power.py` (all pairwise merges; `data/xai/effects_pt2_groupings*`), `manual_phase7_pt2c_overlap_grouping.py` (`data/xai/effects_pt2c_overlap_grouping.json`). Framework: `tsxai.effects.sensitivity` (analytic tests in `tsxai/tests/test_effects.py`). Reading rules were written in the scripts' docstrings **before** any episode was run.

## 1. Design

* **Players** (the benchmark's probe levels, conventions and not agronomic values): heat wave +8 degC (hours 48-96 of the window), high-radiation spell x1.3 (72-120, overlapping the heat wave), cold wave -6 degC (200-248), wind spell x3 (150-186). Reference: the unperturbed run. Valuation: the outcome with exactly the players of the coalition ON. Policies: REACTIVE, and REPLAYED actuation (the reference's commands imposed). Windows: tomato and pepper from hour 960, lettuce from hour 336 (its campaign lasts 42 days).
* **Outcomes** (thresholds read from the crop catalogue for the stage of the window's month, with their source): heating and cooling energy, hours above the stage's maximum temperature, hours above its VPD limit, peak temperature. A cell is MATERIAL when the absolute contrast reaches 1 kWh, 1 h or 0.05 K (declared conventions). 54 of the 90 (episode, policy, outcome) cells are material: reactive cooling, heating and VPD hours; replayed temperature hours, VPD hours and peak. Energy under replay is zero by construction and the temperature under the reactive policy is masked (below), so those cells carry nothing.
* **Sensitivities:** S1 the interaction-free score `v({i}) - v(empty)` (what an analysis that takes one intervention at a time computes, as Weilbach et al. 2024 do by design) against the exact share; S2 grouping (merge heat wave and radiation; split the heat wave into two adjacent halves, a pure regrouping: checked, the all-on scenario is identical); S3 level (all perturbations at 0.5x and 1.5x; offsets scaled, multipliers scaled in their excess over 1); S4 reference (a dull period, radiation x0.8 over the whole window, already present; events compose in the twin: temperature offsets add, radiation multipliers multiply).
* **Checks, all met in the nine episodes:** efficiency of every division (maximum residual 4.5e-13), replaying the source's own commands reproduces the source run in every query, the split heat wave is the same scenario as the single one. Tomato seed 0 reproduces PT-1's numbers exactly (a cross-check between two scripts). Each episode took about 240 s (tomato, pepper) or 120 s (lettuce): 174 runs and 46 cache hits.

## 2. Results against the reading rules

| Rule | Result (54 material cells unless stated) |
|---|---|
| **R1 masking** of the peak temperature, |reactive contrast| / |replayed contrast| | **9 of 9 episodes "masked"** (ratio at most 1e-6, a numerical zero). The peak under the reactive controller equals the crop's upper set-point in every episode (lettuce 20, pepper 25, tomato 26 degC), also at 0.5x, 1.5x and with the dull reference (27 further variants, reactive contrast below 0.0001 K in all). Under replay the same perturbations raise the peak by 6.3-6.4 K (lettuce), 8.8-8.9 K (pepper), 9.0-9.2 K (tomato) and add 19-27 hours above the stage maximum. ## 3. How to read it, and what it does not say

* **The masking is a property of the equipment convention, not of greenhouses.** The illustrative cooling capacity (0.45 kW per m2 of floor, `src/xai_benchmark_design.json`) never saturates, even with a 12 K heat wave and radiation x1.45; a controller that holds the set-point exactly leaves no trace of the perturbation in the temperature, only in the energy. Whether masking survives a smaller capacity is exactly the open question (PT-3: sweep the capacity and find where it breaks, then ask whether data-only methods see the perturbation when the temperature shows nothing).
* **"The top player does not change" is a weak test here.** In these perturbations one player dominates each outcome (the heat wave in five of them, the cold wave for heating), so agreement of the first place is partly a consequence of dominance. The informative figure is the order of the rest (tau-b down to 0.18) and the size of the shares.
* **Ignoring interactions cost little in this family:** at most 7.4 % of the contrast and never a different first place. This agrees with a design whose only interacting pair is heat wave and radiation, where the interaction is small (median gap 5 %). It is not a general justification for dropping interactions: PT-2c below shows a design where they matter.
* **Grouping: zero here by structure, material where the game is coupled.** In PT-2's design the cold wave and the wind spell are additive or null, so the only interacting pair is heat wave and radiation, and merging inside an isolated pair keeps that sub-game's total: all 324 pairwise merges of PT-2b (every pair, 54 material cells, both policies) give a discrepancy of at most 8e-8. That is a property of the design. PT-2c (tomato, seed 0) makes three perturbations overlap in the same 48 hours (heat wave +8, radiation x1.3, cold wave -6, whose offset adds to the heat wave's): **12 of 15 merges exceed 10 % of the contrast, up to 41.5 %**. The three merges of one outcome give the same number: in a three-player game the discrepancy of any merge is one sixth of the third-order interaction, symmetric in the players (derived by hand, not checked in code). Consequence: a grouping must be declared and its sensitivity reported whenever three or more perturbations can overlap.
* **S3 and S4 vary one thing at a time and keep the rest.** A different weather, another crop's set-points or other equipment are other questions. The level scaling does not search for the level at which the controller saturates.
* **Limits shared with PT-1:** development partition, uncalibrated crop model, levels and equipment are conventions, no real-system validation, EnergyPlus not used here. Time costs are measurements of this machine and are not part of any report.

## 4. What PT-2 answers and what it opens

* The question of PT-1 (is the effect of the controller general or was it one episode?) has a first answer in this family: in all nine episodes the reactive controller leaves no trace of the perturbation on the temperature and pays in energy; with its commands held, the same perturbations raise the peak by 6-9 K. The generality is limited to what the equipment convention allows.
* Open and ordered by what resolves most uncertainty: (1) capacity sweep, to find where masking breaks (and with it, where a data-based method could see anything); (2) the family with overlapping players as the default design, since that is where grouping and interactions matter; (3) the declared causal graph and the first data-based methods (7.5), which need exactly those episodes; (4) approximate Shapley for more than 8 players only if a measurement asks for it (a run costs about 1.4 s).
